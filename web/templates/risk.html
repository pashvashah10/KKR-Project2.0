Where the exposure sits.

Each cell is a day, shaded by the share of that day's contribution margin the model expects to lose to {{ risk.peril|lower if risk.peril else 'weather' }}. Shading is share rather than pounds on purpose — absolute exposure just traces your booking volume, and would hide the days that are unusually exposed for what they are worth.

What to act on

Three weeks carry most of it.

Ninety numbers is not a decision. These are the contiguous weeks carrying the most exposure — the resolution at which you can actually move a promotion, a rota or a marketing spend.

A word on what this is: every day here is drawn from the fitted climatological distribution for that day of year, not from a forecast. Past about ten days a forecast has no skill, and this window runs to {{ risk.days }} — which is precisely the horizon at which you commit spend. Climatology is not a weaker substitute there. It is the only instrument that exists.

How this is computed

  • Two thousand simulated seasons of the fitted generator, over the next {{ risk.days }} days at this venue's own coordinates
  • Your loss curve converts each day's {{ risk.variable|replace('_', ' ') }} into a share of a normal day's margin lost
  • That share is applied to what is on the books for that date, so a quiet Tuesday and a sold-out Saturday differ by more than the weather
