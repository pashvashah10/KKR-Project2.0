{% endif %} {# --------------------------------------------------------- uncertainty #} {% if uncertainty_svg %}

Where the uncertainty lives

Three sources, not one.

Internal variability is the weather being weather. It dominates the near term and is roughly constant in absolute terms, so as the other sources grow its share falls.

Parameter uncertainty is the model's doubt about its own coefficients. Scenario uncertainty is which future humanity picks — nil at short horizons, dominant by the end.

The practical consequence: a contract two years out is priced almost entirely off weather noise, so history is nearly sufficient. A contract in 2075 is priced off scenario spread, and quoting it from historical frequencies is simply wrong.

Extremes

The tail, fitted properly.

Everything a weather product pays on lives in the tail, and a Gaussian fitted to the body understates how often the tail is reached. This is a generalised Pareto fit to peaks over a {{ '%.1f'|format(tail.threshold) }} threshold.

Shape {{ '%.3f'|format(tail.shape) }} — positive means heavy and unbounded, which is what rainfall should give; negative means bounded, which is what temperature should give, because physics requires it. Scale {{ '%.2f'|format(tail.scale) }}.
