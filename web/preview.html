A century of weather, turned into a price.

This is live output from the analytics engine, not a mockup. Every number below is computed at run time from a hundred years of daily weather at one coordinate — fitted, projected to 2125 under four emissions pathways, and pushed through to a loss curve.

!

Reading this honestly. The station feed is blocked in this environment, so the record behind these charts is a calibrated surrogate, not observations — built forward from published monthly normals and a physical forcing path. The method is real and every estimator is exercised end to end. The numbers become a real forecast for a real place the moment NOAA is reachable, which is a one-line source swap.

01

The record, and what the model sees in it

Summer maxima at Austin, 1926–2025. The fitted line is not a trend through time — it is the response to modelled global temperature. That is why it reproduces the 1940–75 plateau and the volcanic dips without being told they exist.

02

The next hundred years, by pathway

The same fitted response, driven forward by each SSP forcing path through a two-box energy balance model. The shaded band is the 95% interval on the central pathway — it widens because the far future genuinely is less knowable, not as decoration.

03

Where the uncertainty actually comes from

This is the chart that decides how the book should be priced. Near-dated risk is almost all weather noise, so history is nearly sufficient. Beyond mid-century the dominant unknown is which emissions path the world takes — and no amount of historical data resolves that.

04

The tail, fitted properly

Daily rainfall return levels from a Generalised Pareto fit to exceedances. A Gaussian tail would understate these badly — and the tail is the entire product.

05

The trigger, discovered not asked

Contribution margin regressed on rainfall with a piecewise-linear basis. The curve is flat, then falls off a cliff. Ask an operator and you get "rain hurts us".

06

What it means for a product

Days above 35 °C in a June–August window — the trigger a golf or festival operator would buy against. Today's frequency is not next decade's, and by 2125 the pathway choice is worth — days of payout.

07

The regression, shown not asserted

The fitted mean model. The standard errors are the finding here. Classical OLS treats 36,525 autocorrelated days as independent evidence; a year-block bootstrap says the warming coefficient is —× less precise than that. Every long-dated price widens accordingly.
